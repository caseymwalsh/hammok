What matters for you

  • Capacity. At ~230 lb plus clothes and anything you bring into the hammock, you're near 250 lb. Skip anything rated 250 lb or less, and treat a 250 lb comfort rating as the edge. That rules out the Hennessy Ultralite Backpacker and Hyperlite, the Dream Darien, the single-layer Ridgerunner, and the Chameleon in its lightest fabric.
  • Single vs double layer. A double layer adds 5–10 oz but raises capacity and gives you a sleeve for a pad on cold nights. Warbonnet sells both at the same length.
  • 10 ft vs 11 ft. At 5′7″ a 10 ft hammock gives a flat diagonal lay. 11 ft feels roomier and helps if you're broad, at about 3 oz more.
  • Underquilts. Gathered-end hammocks take almost any gathered-end underquilt. The Wooki only fits the Blackbird and XLC. Bridge hammocks (Ridgerunner, Bear Mountain) need their own bridge underquilt. Lay-flat designs like the Amok and Haven use a sleeping pad instead.
  • All-in-one kits (Hennessy, Kammok Mantis, ENO SubLink and OneLink, Grand Trunk Air Bivy) include a tarp. Their weight looks high next to bare hammocks for that reason. Their tarps are usually smaller than a Journey or Superfly.
  • Compared to what you have. The DoubleNest is a 9′4″ open hammock with no ridgeline, so you lie more curved. Any 10–11 ft hammock with a structural ridgeline will lie flatter, even the cheap ones.
