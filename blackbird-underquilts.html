What matters on a Blackbird

  • Shape. The Blackbird is asymmetric with a footbox, and you lie head-left. Warbonnet's Wooki and Yeti are cut for that lay. Other contoured quilts (Incubator, Zeppelin, Mt Washington) work fine, but you'll spend a few minutes sliding the quilt toward your head end and snugging the suspension the first couple of nights.
  • Length. Get the 10 ft Wooki for the Original Blackbird; the 11 ft one is sized for the XLC. For HG, standard length (78″) suits 5′7″ on a diagonal better than short (73″), even though short is rated up to 5′7″.
  • Width. At ~230 lb you'll sit deeper in the hammock and press more of your shoulders against the sides. The 44″ Incubator and Zeppelin, the 42″ JRB quilts, and the 46″ Zeppelin Wide close drafts at the shoulders better than the 39″ Wooki.
  • ¾ and torso quilts. Phoenix, Greylock, Jarbidge and Yeti save 3–8 oz but need a pad under your legs. A torso quilt plus sit pad usually weighs about the same as a full quilt, so the main gain is pack space.
  • Multi-use quilts (Hudson River, Old Rag, Firebelly) double as top quilts. They're flat rectangles, so on a Blackbird they gap more than a contoured underquilt. Good if you want one quilt for hammock and ground.
  • What to skip. ENO and Onewind quilts are built for wide gathered-end hammocks like the DoubleNest, run 24–40 oz, and pack to 9–12 L. That's no step up from the Wise Owl you're borrowing.
  • Lead times. Warbonnet ships in about 1–2 weeks; Hammock Gear quotes about 20 business days on custom builds. Check HG's in-stock page for faster options.
